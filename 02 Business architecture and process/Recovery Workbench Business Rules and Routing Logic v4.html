How a case is routed

Every move a case makes is one rule. When an event arrives at a case in a given state and the condition holds, the Workbench takes the action, moves the case to the next state, gives it to the named owner and starts the timer. There are 448 rules in ten route sets.

Rules448

The decision table

Each route set is one decision table in Decision Model and Notation 1.4.

ColumnKindMeaning
RuleIdentifierR, the route set code and a number, for example R-LIT-12
From stateInputThe state the case is in when the event arrives
EventInputWhat happened: a payment, a document, a decision, a date reached, a partner result
ConditionInputWhat must hold for the rule to fire. Any means the event alone fires it
ActionOutputWhat the Workbench does
To stateOutputThe state the case moves to
OwnerOutputWho holds the case in the next state. Workbench means no person
SystemsOutputThe systems the action reads or writes
TimerOutputHow long the next state may last before it escalates
Hit policy: UniqueIn a route set, for one state and one event, at most one condition holds, so one rule fires.

Rules held as data

Every rule is a row in a versioned table, with its rule ID, version, valid from, valid to, author, approver and test case ID. A rule change is a validated data load, not a code release. A case keeps the rule version it was decided under, and the decision log records the rule ID and version for every move.

What decides the route

TestWhat it decides
ProductMortgage, hire purchase or SSME. The product decides which routes are open at all.
Days past due and exposureWhich band the case falls in, and therefore who owns it and what they may approve.
SecurityWhether the bank holds an asset, a guarantee or nothing.
Hard gatesA death, fraud, bankruptcy, a complaint or a legal hold stops the normal route and sends the case to a deviation owner first.

The eight routes

RouteChosen whenHow it runs
RestructureThe customer can pay, but needs longer terms.COLLINS proposes, FCR builds the offer.
SettlementThe customer can pay a reduced amount now.Priced against the payoff floor.
Deed in lieuThe customer will hand over the asset instead of paying.Valued, consented, transferred at the Land Office.
Guarantee claimA guarantor or an insurer covers part or all of it.Claim filed with TCG or the insurer.
LitigationNo engagement, or the amount justifies court.Notices, then a panel law firm, then judgment.
Asset disposalThe bank already holds the asset.Listed and run down the price ladder.
Debt saleThe account is worth more sold than worked.Built into a lot and awarded to a buyer.
Write offNothing is left to recover economically.Proposed monthly, approved, posted.

How a case ends

EndWhat happens
Cured or settledThe customer pays, a plan runs to term, or a settlement clears.
RealizedAn asset sells, a guarantee pays, or the debt is sold.
Written offPosted and closed, with recovery continuing after charge-off.

Routes by product

ProductRoutes openWhere the volume goes
MortgageRestructure, settlement, deed in lieu, litigation, asset disposal, debt sale, write offDeed in lieu and asset disposal carry the volume. The Land Office transfer and the LED auction ladder are the long steps.
Hire purchaseRestructure, settlement, voluntary surrender, litigation, asset disposal, debt sale, write offVoluntary surrender replaces deed in lieu. The asset moves faster and the auction cycle is shorter.
SSMERestructure, settlement, guarantee claim, litigation, asset disposal, debt sale, write offThe guarantee claim route matters here because of TCG cover. Deed in lieu applies only where there is property security.
UnsecuredSettlement, litigation, debt sale, write offNo asset, so no disposal and no deed in lieu. Most volume ends in debt sale or write off.

A deviation stops the normal route at any point and returns the case when it clears. The universal rules run in every state.

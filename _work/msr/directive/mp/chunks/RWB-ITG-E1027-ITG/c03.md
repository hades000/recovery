## 5. GAP-1237 (Register gap; ruling R-22043; sections: find it)
- Title: The canonical form of the entry hash is not specified
- Detail: The module specification fixes SHA-256 over the canonical form of the entry and the first previous hash as 64 zeros, and the design names prev_hash and entry_hash, but no source fixes the field order, null handling, encoding, separators or digest rendering of that canonical form. Two writers could compute different digests for the same record and the nightly chain check would then report a false break.
- Evidence: FSD P7 A2.1 AC-ALT-P7-001-01: every entry_hash equals the SHA-256 of the entry's canonical form, first prev_hash 64 zeros. Temporal and Drools Design slide 16 lists the record fields and prev_hash/entry_hash with no canonicalisation rule.
- Proposed fix: Freeze the canonical form in the contract baseline (ordered field list, UTF-8, no whitespace, null rendered as an empty string, 64 lowercase hex) and publish one cross-language fixture that the store verifier and every writer must reproduce.
- Owner role: Solution architect

## 8. GAP-1622 (Register gap; ruling R-28458; sections: find it)
- Title: The decision record is published by 19 services on one topic with no schema version field in the field list
- Detail: Nineteen services publish DecisionRecorded on one shared topic and this service consumes it into a single ledger. The field list of decision_log_entry has no schema version and no producer version, and the CloudEvents envelope carries a type and a source but no contract version. A field added by one producer, or a rule version whose format changes, would reach the consumer with no way to tell which contract the producer used, and the ledger would hold mixed shapes without saying so.
- Evidence: Arch v5 slide 42 row 08 reads 'DecisionRecorded / all 19 / rwb.decision-record.v1 / rwb-decision, rwb-reporting'; TSD P7 T3.1 lists DF-DEC-001 to DF-DEC-031 with no schema or producer version; DP-09 sets CloudEvents for every event.
- Proposed fix: Add a contract version to the DecisionRecorded event in the event and messaging design, record it on the ledger entry, and let the consumer accept a known set of versions and send an unknown one to the dead-letter topic rather than guessing.
- Owner role: Platform event library owner

## 9. GAP-1617 (Register gap; ruling R-28459; sections: find it)
- Title: The ledger completeness target, the gap alert threshold and the strict-mode wait bound have no value in the BRD or the FSD
- Detail: Architecture v5 states that rwb-decision reads the decision-record topic into the ledger within five minutes, raises gap alerts and verifies the chain, and that strict mode waits for the ledger acknowledgement at four points of no return. Neither the BRD's auditability requirements nor the FSD gives a completeness target, an alert threshold or a bound on the strict-mode wait, and the technical specification does not carry one either. Without a value the ingest cannot be sized, the alert cannot be tested and a caller cannot say how long a point of no return may wait.
- Evidence: Arch v5 slide 28 note reads 'rwb-decision consumes it idempotently into the central ledger, with a 5-minute completeness target, gap alerts and chain verification'; the same slide says strict mode steps 'wait until the central ledger confirms the record' with no bound; BRD NFR-033 to NFR-035 state r ...
- Proposed fix: Set the completeness target, the gap alert threshold and the maximum strict-mode wait as one reference-data row each, proposed as the five minutes of Architecture v5 for the target and the alert, and confirm the wait bound with Risk and Internal Audit at the ADR-022 sign-off.
- Owner role: Solution architect with the platform administrator

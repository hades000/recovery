1. The records
The Workbench keeps the seven records of the source, and the structure of the source is kept with three sections added at the end (ASM-B-TECH-12-3). In the target the decision log is written twice by design: a local decision record in the schema of the service that took the decision, written in the same transaction as the state change, and the same entry in the central ledger of rwb-decision (P7), collected from the DecisionRecorded event on topic rwb.decision-record.v1 (ADR-022; Architecture v5 slides 20 and 28). The interaction ledger stays with rwb-source-data (P1), the access log moves to rwb-access (P4) and the application logs are collected per service. Every row of the source is kept; the Where column carries the store of the target.
Record | What it holds | Form | Where | Read by
Decision log | Every decision: inputs, rule and model versions, recommendation, approver, action | Append only, hash chained, one entry per decision | The local record in the schema of the service that took the decision, sealed into the central ledger in rwb_decision (P7); the nightly copy to the data lake is removed (ADR-022, ADR-018; DP-03, DP-17; CG-B-TECH-12-5; ASM-B-TECH-12-8) | Internal audit, Risk, center of excellence
Interaction ledger | Every contact, letter, promise, payment and hand-over | Append only, one entry per interaction | rwb_source_data (P1), the ledger owner of record; rwb-case and the other services read it through the P1 contract (DP-18; CG-B-TECH-12-6; ASM-B-TECH-12-7) | Case owners in scope; Conduct and QA
Access log | Sign-ins, refusals, grants, removals, break-glass, versions in force | Append only | rwb_access (P4), its own store, served by GET /admin/access-log (DP-08) | Application owner, security administrator, Compliance, internal audit
ACE audit log | Every change made through the service | ACE pattern | ACE logging | Security administrator, internal audit
PII log | Every read and unmask of a personal field | Event per read | PII Kafka | Compliance, internal audit
SOC log | Security events | Event per incident | SCB security operations | Security operations
Application logs | Errors, warnings and traces with no personal data | Structured JSON | Log Analytics, one stream per service, collected by the observability platform (PLT) (ADR-023) | Platform administrator, squads
One record, one owner. The decision record is the only record with more than one writer: each of the 19 services writes its own, in its own schema, and rwb-decision (P7) holds the central ledger. Section 7 names the owner, the store and the contract of every record.
2. The decision log entry
Version 3 keeps the eleven fields of the entry. The entry is written once, in one transaction with the state change and the outbox row (R-UNI-39), and it is then collected, not rewritten, in the central ledger. The table is the table of the source; the target names of the same information are given in 2.1.
Field | Meaning
entry_id, case_id | The entry and its case
decision_code | D1 to D9, a sub-decision or a deviation
rule_id, rule_version | The rule that fired and its version
model_version, agent_version, prompt_version | Where a model or agent took part
inputs | References to the data and its business date, not copies of personal data
recommendation, reasons | What was proposed and why
decided_by, approval_level, limit_applied | Who decided, at which level, under which limit
action | What the Workbench did
previous_hash, hash | The chain that shows no entry has been changed
corrects_entry_id | Set when the entry corrects an earlier one (R-UNI-24)
2.1 Field correspondence with the target record
The target record carries the fields in the second column, as the owning service writes them in its own schema (Temporal and Drools Design v1; Architecture v5 slide 28). The source field names are kept in the first column so that a reader of the two versions can follow one entry through.
Source field | Target field | Note
entry_id, case_id | chain_seq, with the case reference and the trace ID held in inputsRef | The entry ID of the source becomes the position of the entry in the chain of its service; the record has no separate case column (CG-B-TECH-12-2; ASM-B-TECH-12-4)
decision_code | ruleId with ruleVersion, and outcome | D1 to D9 is the outcome of the decision
rule_id, rule_version | ruleId, ruleVersion | The rule that fired and the version in force
model_version, agent_version, prompt_version | ruleVersion, plus the model and prompt versions inside inputsRef | One version per artefact; the list stays as the source writes it
inputs | inputsRef | References to the data and its business date, never a copy of personal data
recommendation, reasons | recommendation, outcome | What was proposed and what was decided
decided_by, approval_level, limit_applied | approverUserId, approvalLevel, finalCall | The limit applied is read from rwb-access (P4) and the row is kept
action | actions | What the Workbench did
previous_hash, hash | prev_hash, entry_hash | The chain of the local record; the ledger chains the entry again on ingest
corrects_entry_id | the reference to the entry it corrects | Append only: the original stays (R-UNI-24)
The target record adds decidedAt, the moment of the decision, and the trace ID of section 3. Neither is a new business value.
3. Tracing an action end to end
Every request carries a trace ID from the front end through the service, the outbox and the outbound file.
The decision log entry stores the trace ID.
The interaction ledger entry stores the decision log entry ID where a decision caused it.
Outbound files carry the case number and the entry ID, so a notice in WIN can be traced to its decision.
Internal audit searches by case, rule, approver or date on the decision log query screen and exports with masking.
Version 3 adds three points, because one action now crosses services:
Each service propagates the W3C Trace Context header, so one trace covers the 19 services, the Kafka events and the Temporal workflow of the case (Architecture v5 slide 49; ADR-023).
The local decision record carries the trace ID and the chain position, and the ledger entry keeps both, so a ledger entry is traced back to the workflow run that wrote it. The case reference travels in inputsRef (2.1).
The audit search is answered by rwb-decision (P7) on GET /decision-log and GET /decision-log/{entryId}; the export applies the masking rows of rwb-access (P4) (DP-08).
4. Integrity
The decision log, the interaction ledger and the access log accept inserts only. Migrations cannot update or delete their rows.
A daily job checks the hash chain and reports a break to Compliance the same day.
A correction is a new entry that names the entry it corrects; the original stays.
A failed write to the decision log stops the action; a failed write to the ledger raises an incident.
Version 3 keeps those four rules and says where each one runs:
The daily chain check is JOB-14, a Temporal Schedule in rwb-decision (P7). The ingest that feeds it is JOB-13, continuous over Kafka in the same service (Architecture v5 slide 44; DP-10).
The local record, the state change and the outbox row are one transaction in the schema of the owning service. If the write fails, nothing moves (R-UNI-39; Temporal and Drools Design v1).
Strict mode at the points of no return: debt transfer, write-off posting, auction sale and title transfer wait for the confirmation of the central ledger before they execute. Every other step carries on at full speed while the ledger catches up (Architecture v5 slides 26 and 28; ASM-B-TECH-12-10).
The ledger is anchored daily: a fingerprint of the ledger chain is written to write-once storage, and a missing record raises an alert inside the five-minute completeness target (Architecture v5 slides 14 and 28).
The chain of the local record and the chain of the ledger are both verified; what an auditor treats as the chain of record is still open (CG-B-TECH-12-8; ASM-B-TECH-12-10).
While the ledger lags, the local record is authoritative for the audit trail and the ledger entry is shown as not yet sealed; the audit search of section 3 reads the ledger only (CG-B-TECH-12-9; ASM-B-TECH-12-16).
5. Retention
Record | Retention
Decision log | Set by Compliance (DL26); proposed as the life of the case plus the bank records period
Interaction ledger | Set by Compliance, separately from the decision log (DL26)
Access log | Per the bank retention schedule (DL26)
Application logs | 90 days in Log Analytics, then archived
The periods of the source are unchanged. Version 3 records where the retention data sits: retention_rule and retention_action are tables of rwb-document (P5) and JOB-17 is the retention Schedule in rwb-document; that is document retention, not ledger retention. No source states where the retention period of the decision ledger, of the interaction ledger and of the access log is held, or which job enforces it (CG-B-TECH-12-4; ASM-B-TECH-12-9). The access log is retained with its own store in rwb_access (P4); the application logs are retained per service in Log Analytics (ADR-023).
6. What is never logged
Clear national ID or passport numbers.
Personal fields in application logs, traces or agent prompts.
Secrets, tokens or keys.
Version 3 adds three rules of the same kind, because the decision record now travels as an event:
Personal data in Kafka events. The events carry references, and a payload is encrypted by the codec before it leaves the service (ADR-015; T1; ASM-B-TECH-12-13).
Personal data in workflow inputs or workflow history. The payloads are encrypted by the codec and the workflow holds the case ID and references (ADR-016).
Personal fields in the inputs of a decision record, which hold references and the business date only (section 2).
The approver of a decision is recorded as an identifier, not as a name, and the field is masked in an export under the masking rows of rwb-access (CG-B-TECH-12-7; ASM-B-TECH-12-11).

7.1 Dependencies
The local record needs the decision-record library of the platform, a contract story of wave 0: H RWB-PLT-E1027-CON (DP-03). The same library writes the outbox row: H RWB-PLT-TE006-CON (DP-09).
A service that searches the central ledger depends on the contract of rwb-decision as a C dependency. A C dependency never blocks a build: the service builds against the published contract and its fake (Rules v1 R5.4).
The access log is reached through the authorise contract of rwb-access: H RWB-PLT-E1037-CON and C RWB-P4-E1037-CON (DP-08).
The interaction ledger is reached through the P1 contract: C against RWB-P1 (DP-18). No service reads another service's ledger, access-log or decision-record table (DP-01).

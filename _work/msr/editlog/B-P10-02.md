## What was done

The WP was executed with fanout 5: one task subagent (aisdlc-msr-worker) per legacy key, launched in one message, each owning only that key's two or three story files, its fragments and its own jsonl rows. No legacy file, master file, plan file or other WP's output was edited, and no id was renumbered — only step D1 numbers rows as GAP-nnnn and ASM-nnnn.

The WP had been left in `RUNNING` (tries 2) by two earlier attempts that wrote the thirteen story files, the thirteen fragments and the per-key register rows but never merged the registers and never closed the WP. This pass finished it: the per-key audit was re-run for all five keys, three keys were blocked by the provider rate limit and the workspace concurrency ceiling and were audited by the WP owner instead, the five per-key register files were merged into the two WP-level registers, and the per-key working copies were removed.

The completed per-key audits (RWB-TE-013 and RWB-E2-028) found and fixed real defects, all of the same family: story text and fragments cited the `B2-P10-00x` register of the later split queue instead of this WP's own rows. Those seventeen and twenty-eight citations were remapped, by subject, to this WP's own gap and assumption ids, each carrying its legacy key in the middle of the id; the OPS title line of RWB-E2-028 lost a file-name suffix, one "six/eight scenarios" count and two operation counts in RWB-TE-013 were corrected, and the section 18 paragraphs were rewritten to name the rows that actually settle each default. The same defect was then found and fixed by the WP owner in RWB-E2-029, RWB-E2-030 and RWB-E2-031, where six defaults had no row in this register and were added as new assumption rows (E2029-11 to -13, E2030-13 to -15, E2031-14 to -17) with the same substance as the split-queue rows.

After the work every one of the thirteen story files cites this WP's rows only: no split-queue citation remains, every gap and assumption id cited in a story resolves in the two merged registers, and every fragment lists exactly the rows its story cites.
